The Story

From the birthplace of black tea

Wild Mountain Golden Needle is sourced from abandoned, semi-wild tea gardens deep within the Tongmuguan core zone of Wuyi Mountain — the birthplace of Lapsang Souchong and, by extension, the origin of all black tea in the world.

The tea shrubs here, some over fifty years old, grow untended among bamboo groves and pine forests at high elevation. They are harvested only once a year, in early spring, when the first tender buds emerge from months of winter dormancy. Each batch is hand-plucked by local tea-farming families and finished by a master tea maker using the exacting Jin Jun Mei technique — withering, rolling, controlled oxidation, and a slow, low-temperature roast over charcoal.

Tasting Notes

Four dimensions of character

🍂

Dry Leaf

Slender, uniformly tight buds, each dressed in fine golden down. Jet black with brilliant gold tips — the hallmark of genuine, well-crafted Jin Jun Mei. No broken pieces. No stems. Pure buds only.

👃

Aroma

The dry leaf opens with roasted sweet potato, dark honey, and a whisper of wildflowers. Once warmed in the gaiwan, it deepens into layers of cacao, dried longan, and a faint pine-resin note — a signature of high-mountain tea grown in pristine conditions.

✨

Liquor

Luminous amber-orange, crystal-clear and heavy-bodied. The liquor clings to the cup walls — a reliable indicator of rich soluble content and high-elevation leaf quality.

👅

Flavor

  • 1st steep (95°C / 203°F, 5s): Silky and round. Honey sweetness with hints of ripe stone fruit.
  • 2nd–4th: The body builds. Dark cacao, malt, and baked sweet potato emerge, balanced by clean minerality. Zero astringency.
  • 5th–8th: Gentle, lingering sweetness with a refreshing aftertaste. The brew remains full-bodied — never thins.
